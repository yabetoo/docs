A refund on a Connect charge is requested through the **ordinary refund route** — there is no
dedicated Connect endpoint. What changes is the way the funds are recovered.

## The policy: Yabetoo never advances funds

A seller's wallet cannot be debited after the fact. A negative balance would be
a permanently unrecoverable debt.

<Warning>
**If the money is not there, the refund fails.** Yabetoo does not create debt and does not let
any wallet go negative. This is a "no credit" policy, and it is visible in the API:
`402 connect.insufficient_funds_for_refund`.
</Warning>

## How the funds are recovered

Three tiers, in this order, each capped at its own share:

<Steps>
  <Step title="The seller's pending balance">
    Funds that have not matured yet are seized first — that is precisely what the availability
    delay is for.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The seller's available balance">
    For the remainder of their share.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your balance">
    Your share of the refund, **plus everything the seller could not cover**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
**You are the payer of last resort.** If the seller has already withdrawn their funds, it is
your balance that absorbs the difference. This is the opposite of an
[allocation reversal](/en/connect/payments/allocations), where the shortfall is simply
refused.
</Warning>

Recovery is **capped per charge**: we never seize more, on a given charge, than that charge
brought in. Without this cap, a refund would dip into the funds from the seller's other sales.

## What Yabetoo does not return

<Warning>
**Yabetoo's fees — collection and the Connect surcharge — are not refundable.** They stay
earned in either mode.
</Warning>

In `controller` mode, on a full refund of 10,000: the seller returns exactly what they
received, and you return your entire commission even though you had only kept the residue. So
you end up negative by your Yabetoo fees over the round trip. This is not an added penalty: it
is the direct consequence of the fact that you had already absorbed them at capture.

## The refusal — `402`

```json 402
{
  "status": 402,
  "code": "connect.insufficient_funds_for_refund",
  "message": "refunding this charge needs 10000 from the marketplace wallet, which only holds 3000 (the seller could only cover 0 of it)",
  "required": 10000,
  "available": 3000,
  "shortfall": 7000,
  "seller_available": 0,
  "marketplace_balance": 3000,
  "currency": "xaf"
}
```

<ResponseField name="required" type="number">What has to be taken from your balance.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="seller_available" type="number">What the seller was able to cover.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="shortfall" type="number">What is missing.</ResponseField>

The check and the debit happen in **a single transaction**, with locks held: a concurrent
withdrawal cannot slip in between the two.

<Note>
**All or nothing.** There is no automatic partial refund: a buyer who asks for 10,000 and
receives 4,000 without having chosen it is a worse dispute than the refusal.
</Note>

## Reacting to a refusal

The refusal is not silent: it emits
[`connect.refund.blocked_insufficient_funds`](/en/connect/webhooks), with the same amounts.

<Warning>
Without this webhook, you would discover the `402` through an unhappy buyer. Subscribe to it
from day one.
</Warning>

To top your balance back up, use a deposit — it goes through a Yabetoo validation, so plan for
the delay.

## Reducing your exposure

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Lengthen the availability delay">
    This is the main lever. Pending funds are seizable; withdrawn funds are not. A shorter
    delay shifts the risk onto your own balance.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do not pay out immediately after maturation">
    In manual mode, you decide when to pay. Holding a few extra days on sellers with a high
    dispute rate mechanically reduces your exposure.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Track your 402 rate">
    That is the number that tells you whether your delay is badly calibrated.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
